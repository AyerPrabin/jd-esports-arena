Chapter 01 The Origin
Who I Am

The person behind the system

I was born in Nepal and I'm building in London — and I refuse to let one cancel out the other. My mother gave herself to community service and the arts, and that's the lens I carry into everything: make things that are genuinely useful, make them last, and don't wait for permission to start.

Moving from the hills of Sudurpashchim to a Computing Science degree at Coventry University London was a bigger leap than most people realise. I fund it the hard way — running a bar floor as a manager, pulling warehouse shifts, and feeding every spare pound and hour back into the work. Hospitality taught me what code never could: reading people, de-escalating chaos, and staying calm when everything is on fire at once.

I don't build for résumés. I build systems I actually use. ZULU is the proof — a personal tactical AI I wrote myself, now a 14,000-line operating system running locally on my own machine. No team, no funding. Just a problem worth solving and the discipline to keep showing up.

Alongside it I run AyerFire, my Free Fire esports brand, and JD Esports Arena Nepal — a live tournament platform for the community back home. English, Hindi, Nepali; London and the mountains; the digital and the physical. The whole point is that I operate across all of it at once.

Chapter 03 The Vision
The Long Game

Build in London, plant it in Nepal

The work here — the degree, the shifts, the systems I build after midnight — is the launchpad. The destination is home: turning a family stake in the Sudurpashchim hills into the foundation of something durable, built on real assets and patient, sequenced execution. The detailed plan stays close to my chest. What's public is the direction: from operator to owner, from London to the mountains.
